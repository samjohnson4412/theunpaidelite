These are ordered by what actually recovers money, not by what feels satisfying. If you only do one thing, do the first one.

Before anything else: get the company’s details right

Complaints and lawsuits filed against a misspelled company can be rejected or dismissed. The correct legal name is Elite CX Solutions, LLC, doing business as Elite CXS — C X S, not C S X.

Before you file anything, look the company up on the Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) to confirm its current status and its registered agent’s name and address. The registered agent is who gets served in a lawsuit, and that information can change.

Small claims court

Most effective

No lawyer needed · filing fee typically $30–$100 · weeks to a few months

This is the one that has actually produced money. A contractor sued Elite CXS in Pasco County, Florida and won a judgment — then had to file a motion for contempt before payment arrived. It still arrived.

Nearly every claim in our records is under $2,500, which is small claims territory everywhere in the country. The process is deliberately built for people without lawyers: you file a short form, the company is served, and you show a judge your invoices and emails.

Where to file

  • Pasco County, Florida — where the company is based. Always an option, and the court where the existing judgment was obtained. Florida allows small claims up to $8,000.
  • Your own county — often possible when the work was performed there, and far more convenient. Rules on suing an out-of-state company vary, so ask your clerk of court before you file.

We cannot prepare your case for you. Nobody here is a lawyer, and filling out someone else’s court paperwork is not something we are able to do. What we can give you is the company’s verified details, the figures from this site, and what other contractors have run into.

Your clerk of court is the right place for the forms themselves. Most clerks have a self-help page or a counter where staff will show you which form to use and how to serve it — that help is free, and it is their job.

Your state’s labor agency

Free, and underused

Free to file · the agency does the work · months

Most states have a labor commissioner or wage-and-hour division that investigates unpaid wage claims, at no cost to you. At least one contractor has already filed a wage claim against Elite CXS with the California Labor Commissioner.

Companies often argue that mystery shoppers are independent contractors and therefore outside these agencies’ reach. Sometimes that argument loses — several states apply a strict test for who really counts as a contractor, and an agency that has received complaints from several states starts to see a pattern. It costs you nothing to file, so file.

Search for “[your state] labor commissioner wage claim” or “[your state] department of labor unpaid wages complaint.”

Attorney general complaints

Builds the pattern

Free · 15 minutes · no direct payout to you

An attorney general will not collect your individual debt. What they can do is open an investigation into a pattern — and a pattern is exactly what this is. File in two places:

  • Florida, where the company operates — myfloridalegal.com
  • Your own state, where you performed the work and were harmed

Police report

Mixed results

Free · usually must be filed by phone or in person

Some contractors have reported this to police as theft of services — the argument being that the company obtained work, and required out-of-pocket purchases, while intending not to pay. Whether a department treats that as a crime or as a civil matter varies enormously, and many will tell you it is civil.

It is still worth doing, for two reasons: a report number is useful supporting material for other complaints, and reports accumulating in the company’s home county are noticed.

  • File where you performed the shops, or in Pasco County, Florida, where the company is based.
  • Explain that you completed approved work, were promised reimbursement for purchases you funded yourself, and were not paid.
  • Bring your invoice and your unanswered emails.

If you got a 1099 for money you never received

Protects you

Do this before you file that year’s return

Several contractors report receiving a 1099 reporting income that was never actually paid to them. If that happened to you, do not ignore it — the IRS has been told you were paid, and will expect tax on it.

  • Ask the company in writing for a corrected 1099. Keep the request even if you get no reply — it documents that you tried.
  • Talk to a tax preparer before filing. There is a normal way to report income you did not receive, and doing it correctly protects you.
  • Keep your proof of non-payment with your tax records.

This is genuinely a question for a tax professional, not for us.

Public record

Slow burn

Free · helps the next person more than it helps you

Complaints to the Better Business Bureau and reviews on mystery shopping forums and job boards will not get you paid. They do keep the next contractor from taking an assignment they will not be paid for, and they leave a public trail that reporters and investigators find later. Post the facts, keep it accurate, and keep your receipts.

Watch your deadlines

Every one of these routes has a time limit, and they differ by state and by claim type — some wage claims expire in as little as a year. The oldest work in our records dates to 2020. If your work is a few years back, check your deadline now rather than later, and consider talking to a lawyer.
