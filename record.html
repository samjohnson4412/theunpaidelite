Response

What people were told

The same explanations recur across contractors who have never met, in different states, years apart. These are quoted from claim forms:

  • That someone had hacked their system — then no further replies.
  • That accounting was on vacation and it would be handled on their return.
  • That the owner was in the hospital — reported by a contractor who was given this answer each time she called.
  • That the company had switched payment processors and would be reissuing missed payments. Contractors given this answer in 2024 report never hearing anything further.
  • That the matter had been escalated to the payroll team. Contractors report the payroll team never contacting them.
  • That they needed to register on a new payment platform. Several report completing the signup and still receiving nothing.
  • That there had been an error, now corrected, and payment was being submitted — followed by no payment.

— of — contractors described simply being ignored: no reply at all, or replies that stopped. One reported emailing more than 200 times without a single response.

Two contractors report being threatened

One was told the owner would sue her. Another says she was threatened after reporting the company to Tampa International Airport, where she had performed shops. A third reports being reprimanded by a company representative for asking about the state of the company’s finances after months without payment.

If you are contacted in a way that feels threatening, save it and send it to us. Do not delete it.

Methodology

How these numbers were produced

So that anyone checking our work can see exactly what we did:

  • Source. Claim forms submitted voluntarily by contractors between July 2025 and the date shown above. Nothing here is scraped, purchased or inferred.
  • Self-reported. Amounts are what each contractor says they are owed. We have not independently audited every figure, which is precisely why we are now collecting underlying documents.
  • Deduplicated conservatively. Several people submitted the form more than once. Where submissions matched, we counted the person once and kept a single amount, even where the submissions listed different work dates that may represent separate unpaid shops. This makes the total an understatement rather than an overstatement.
  • One submission has no amount. That person is counted as a contractor but contributes $0 to the total.
  • Recovered claims are excluded. — contractor has been paid in full (—) after a small claims judgment. That amount is reported separately and is not counted as unpaid, here or anywhere else on this site.
  • Geography. Counted by where the work was performed. Where a form named only a city, the contractor’s home state was used.
  • The “ignored” figure counts only forms that explicitly describe no response, silence, or being ignored. Forms describing a reply that led nowhere are not counted, so the true figure is higher.
